The common theme is that an AI-native product should be mapped as a system, not described as a model wrapper.

## Key takeaways

- The AI value chain runs from infrastructure to workflow, distribution, agents, and new economic interfaces.
- Local routing is a latency and cost tool when the decision is frequent, low-risk, and easy to verify.
- Voice, character, memory, and interaction loops are product architecture, not surface decoration.

## Source alignment

The AI value-chain document gives the macro map. The other documents make that map concrete:

- the CPU-only router PoC tests whether small local models can handle speaker selection and minigame plan nodes,
- the WorldX analysis studies an end-to-end AI agent sandbox world,
- the TTS audition evaluates character voice and emotional range,
- the Zopet document records companion-product feel.

Together they point to one design habit: map the entire system before deciding where the model matters.

## The product is the chain

An AI-native product may depend on:

- compute and inference cost,
- model routing,
- proprietary context,
- memory and retrieval,
- agent orchestration,
- workflow distribution,
- user interface,
- feedback loops,
- identity and voice.

The model is one layer. It is not the whole product.

## Local routing is not about replacing large models

The CPU-only router PoC is useful because it asks a narrow question: which decisions can be moved away from the strongest remote model?

Good candidates are:

- frequent,
- latency-sensitive,
- low-risk,
- easy to verify,
- recoverable if wrong.

Speaker selection and lightweight plan-node filtering are different from final response generation. They deserve different latency and cost budgets.

## World simulation needs product hardening

The WorldX research note describes a complete AI agent interactive narrative and sandbox prototype: world generation, maps, characters, LLM-driven actions, dialogue, memory, reflection, and timeline playback.

It also identifies production risks:

- multi-model generation failure recovery,
- simulation concurrency control,
- missing auth boundaries for powerful operations,
- lack of tests, CI, Docker, and deployment boundaries.

That is the system view: the demo can be strong while the production surface remains weak.

## Voice is part of identity

The TTS documents are not only audio tests. They evaluate whether the voice survives real product use.

Useful voice evaluation asks:

- Does the voice match the character's role?
- Does the emotional range hold across common phrases?
- Does repeated use create fatigue?
- Does a happy prefix still sound natural?
- Does the same voice work in neutral, urgent, and playful states?

In a companion product, voice is product identity.
